# Experimental Design
- Strategy: Experiment 1 – Synthetic-Noise Robustness Curve
Objective: Quantitatively verify that R2-D2PO is distributionally robust to both random and adversarial reward corruptions while keeping compute close to DPO.
Design   • Models: Mistral-7B-Instruct (π0) fine-tuned with (i) SFT, (ii) DPO, (iii) LRML, (iv) FIGA, (v) PPO-LoRA, (vi) R2-D2PO.  
  • Data: UltraFeedback + AlpacaFarm (clean) and their NoiseBench versions with total corruption rates {0,10,20,40,60} %; each corruption split contains equal parts (a) uniform flips, (b) empirical crowd confusion, (c) topic-targeted adversarial flips.  
  • Training: 2 epochs, 4-bit QLoRA, 8 × A100-80 GB; R2-D2PO uses K=4 reward ensembles (still <8 % extra FLOPs over DPO).  
  • Evaluation: ① MT-Bench (GPT-4 judge), ② UltraFeedback gold rewards, ③ Safety score (OpenAI), ④ Robustness-AUC = ∫{noise} metric(noise).  
  • Reporting: plot metric vs. noise; tabulate compute vs. performance; run paired bootstrap (10 K) for significance.
What success would show   • Curves for DPO/LRML/FIGA degrade steeply (≥7 MT-Bench pts at 40 %), while R2-D2PO drops ≤2 pts and yields the highest Robustness-AUC (+25 % over next best).  
  • Compute overhead graph shows <10 % extra FLOPs, proving efficiency.  
Demonstrates: The core claim that the new loss preserves clean-data quality yet is markedly more robust under heterogeneous noise without resorting to on-policy RL.

Experiment 2 – Red-Team Jail-Break Stress-Test
Objective: Test the method’s ability to down-weight adversarially corrupted rewards and improve real-world safety/compliance.
Design   • Red-team prompt generator: use GPT-4 + automatic jailbreak search (e.g., JailbreakBench pipeline) to craft 2 000 novel adversarial prompts unseen during training.  
  • Run each model’s policy to answer; score with (i) OpenAI moderation API for policy-violating content, (ii) expert raters (n=5) on compliance & helpfulness.  
  • Metrics: Jail-break success-rate (lower better), Helpful-Compliance F1, Toxicity (Perspective-API).  
  • Ablation: R2-D2PO w/ and w/o disagreement gating (τ=0.2) to isolate its impact.
What success would show   • R2-D2PO reduces jail-break success by ≥30 % vs. DPO and ≥20 % vs. PPO-LoRA while maintaining helpfulness within 0.1 points.  
  • Gating variant adds another 5 % reduction, evidencing on-the-fly trust control.  
Demonstrates: Strength on worst-case adversarial noise and safety gains—an angle that synthetic corruption alone cannot capture.

Experiment 3 – Out-of-Domain Uncertainty & Curriculum Analysis
Objective: Validate that (μ,σ)-based pessimism and risk curriculum adaptively decide how much to trust data and accelerate convergence.
Design   • Domain shift: Fine-tune policies on medical Q&A preferences (MedAlpaca) that are OOD for the reward models.  
  • Instrument training to log: σ of reward ensemble, γ, α schedule, fraction of dropped pairs, and per-step dev MT-Bench score.  
  • Baselines: DPO with identical data, plus ‘Fixed-Risk’ R2-D2PO (γ,α frozen).  
  • Compute calibration metrics: ECE between σ and absolute reward error (gold rewards collected on 500 annotated pairs).  
  • Convergence: plot dev MT-Bench vs. wall-clock hours.
What success would show   • R2-D2PO attains same final score as DPO 1.4× faster because curriculum ignores high-σ pairs early.  
  • σ is well-calibrated (ECE ↓50 % vs. reward variance baseline).  
  • Fixed-Risk variant trains slower and ends lower, confirming curriculum benefit.  
Demonstrates: Method’s ability to estimate epistemic uncertainty, drop or discount untrustworthy data on-the-fly, and learn faster—even when the reward model is clearly OOD.

Collectively, the three experiments cover: (1) stochastic & adversarial robustness, (2) live safety under red-teaming, and (3) uncertainty-aware learning efficiency—corroborating all advertised advantages of R2-D2PO within the provided A100-class compute envelope.
